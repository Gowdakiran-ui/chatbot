## Case 33

**Company:** Delhi High Court right-to-be-forgotten judgment (Laksh Vir Singh v. Union of India and connected petitions)
**Industry:** Search engines, legal databases and online reputation (India)
**Year:** 2026 (judgment dated 29 May 2026; reported 1 June 2026)
**Crisis Type:** Court-ordered de-indexing of old legal records from name searches

**Trigger Event:** A batch of petitions, decided together by Justice Sachin Datta of the Delhi High Court (the report says 31 petitions, W.P.(C) 1021/2016 and connected matters), came from people whose names appeared in search results connected to old court cases, orders and judgments. They asked for the content to be removed from name-based searches.

**Response Type:** Judicial (court direction to search engines and a legal database)

**Response Speed Score:** 2/5 - The lead petition number (W.P.(C) 1021/2016) dates the earliest matter to 2016, while the combined judgment is dated 29 May 2026.

**Transparency Score:** 4/5 - The judgment is a public court document, and the reasoning and the limits of the right are set out.

**Legal / Regulatory Framework Triggered:** Rule 3(1)(d) of the Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021 (intermediaries must remove content when a court orders it or the government issues a notice); Article 21 of the Constitution (right to life, including informational privacy and reputation); Article 226 (the court can direct private parties because fundamental rights can be enforced against non-State actors); Article 19(1)(a) (freedom of speech), which the court said must be balanced against the petitioners' rights.

**Resolution Status:** Resolved (as a ruling) - Google and other search engines were directed to de-index the relevant content, orders, judgments and associated reportage from name-based search results, in the same manner as a direction under Rule 3(1)(d), and to make the de-indexing operate across all versions and domains of the search engine to the extent necessary to protect the petitioners' privacy. Indian Kanoon was directed to restrict name-based search within its platform for the petitioners' records while keeping the judgments accessible by case number, citation, court details and date. The report does not mention an enforcement deadline or an appeal.

**What Went Right:**
- The court set out a route for people affected by old legal records to ask for removal from name searches without erasing the public court record itself
- The ruling kept the records themselves accessible by case number, citation, court and date
- The court set explicit limits: de-indexing may not be appropriate for people convicted of offences against women or children (including under the POCSO Act, 2012) or for breaches of public trust by public servants and elected representatives; where proceedings ended because the defendant died, the right does not apply, although de-indexing may still be considered if online access seriously harms the family, especially children

**What Went Wrong:**
- The lead petition dates to 2016, so some petitioners waited years for a decision
- Names stayed connected to old cases and judgments in search results for as long as the records remained indexed
- The right is limited by explicit exceptions, so not every request can succeed

**Best Practice / What Should Have Happened:** In India, a person whose acquittal or closed case still dominates name searches can seek a court direction to de-index the records from name-based search, citing the privacy and reputation rights in Article 21 and the court-order removal route in Rule 3(1)(d); the claim is likely weaker where one of the court's listed exceptions applies. Keep the case documents (the order that closed the matter, dates) and consider asking the court that issued the original order to mask personal identifiers, while remembering that the public record itself stays accessible.

**Estimated Impact:** Applies to a batch of petitioners; directs Google and all other search engines and the Indian Kanoon legal database; likely to be cited in later requests (the sources reviewed do not report any appeal).

**Onlyne Relevance:** Search Results Management / Legal Takedown / ORM (the Indian precedent for de-indexing outdated legal records from name searches)
